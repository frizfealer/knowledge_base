## Hypothesis
A credit downgrade produces an **isolable, measurable price drop at the event**: around the downgrade date the bond cheapens (yield rises) by more than market and peer drift explain, rather than the move being fully priced in beforehand. The *direction* is assumed backdrop — more credit risk is paid for with a lower price — so the bet is not "does price fall" but "does a distinct, measurable repricing land at the downgrade rather than ahead of it."

## Current vs. proposed
- **Current:** a rating is a public, lagging label — by the time a downgrade is announced the price has already adjusted, so the event itself carries no distinct, isolable price reaction.
- **Proposed:** the downgrade coincides with (or triggers) a measurable downward repricing — the bond cheapens around the event, beyond general market/peer drift, to restore a risk-compensating yield.

## Test
- **Confirms:** around downgrade dates, downgraded bonds show a reliable negative abnormal price move (price down / yield up) versus non-downgraded peers, beyond what the broad market explains.
- **Refutes:** no reliable move at the event — prices had already adjusted beforehand (priced in), or the move is indistinguishable from peer/market noise.
- **Discriminating evidence:** an event study on the downgraded cohort against the full universe as control — abnormal price change in a window bracketing each downgrade date. The originating capture supplies exactly this sample: 134 of ~15k bonds downgraded in 2023 Oct.
